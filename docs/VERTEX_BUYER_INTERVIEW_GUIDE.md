# VERTEX 4D - Buyer Interview Guide

## Purpose

This guide is for validating whether VERTEX can become a paid institutional product.

The goal is not to ask whether people like the idea. The goal is to learn whether a program buyer has a painful, budgeted, recurring problem that VERTEX can solve better than current alternatives.

## Who to interview

Interview 15 to 20 people who manage or buy entrepreneurship program infrastructure.

Prioritize:

- Incubator directors
- Accelerator program directors
- University entrepreneurship center directors
- Economic-development program directors
- Program managers responsible for founder cohorts
- Facilitators or consultants who run cohorts for multiple institutions

Avoid over-weighting independent founders at this stage. Founders are users, but institutions are the most likely first buyers.

## Interview principle

Ask about current behavior, not hypothetical interest.

Weak question:

"Would you use VERTEX?"

Better question:

"Walk me through what happens when a founder enters your program and you need to know whether they are making progress."

## Opening script

"We are testing VERTEX, a decision-readiness system for early-stage founder cohorts. It helps founders move from assumptions to a documented decision by examining the problem, stakeholder system, adoption resistance, economics, and next experiment. We are trying to learn whether this solves a real institutional problem around founder progress, mentor consistency, and program reporting. I am not trying to sell you in this conversation. I want to understand how you manage this today."

## Discovery questions

### Program structure

1. What kind of entrepreneurship program do you run?
2. How many cohorts do you run per year?
3. How many founders or teams are in each cohort?
4. What stage are the founders usually in when they enter?
5. What does a founder need to demonstrate to graduate, receive funding, continue, or move to the next phase?

### Current workflow

6. Walk me through what happens when a new founder enters your program.
7. How do you currently help founders validate assumptions?
8. How do you know whether a founder's thinking improved during the program?
9. How do mentors or facilitators track founder progress?
10. What documents, dashboards, or reports does the program produce today?

### Current pain

11. Where do founders most often get stuck?
12. Where do mentors disagree?
13. Which founder claims are hardest to evaluate?
14. What takes the most staff time?
15. What do you wish you could see across the whole cohort that you cannot see today?
16. What information do you need for funders, sponsors, university leadership, or board reporting?

### Current tools and alternatives

17. What tools do you use today? Examples: Miro, Notion, Excel, LMS, CRM, Strategyzer, Babele, Dealum, AcceleratorApp, ChatGPT, custom templates.
18. What did those tools cost last year?
19. Which tool is most painful to use?
20. Which tool would be hardest to replace?
21. If VERTEX did not exist, what would you use instead?

### Buying process

22. Who owns the budget for program tools or methodology?
23. Who would need to approve a paid pilot?
24. When is budget allocated?
25. What price level can you approve without a long procurement process?
26. What would make adding a new tool impossible right now?

### Reaction to VERTEX

Show only after discovery:

- One complete founder journey
- Before/after example
- Facilitator dashboard
- DecisionRecord sample

Then ask:

27. Which part feels most valuable?
28. Which part feels unnecessary?
29. What would have to be true for you to run this in your next cohort?
30. What evidence would you need before paying?
31. Would this replace something, add to something, or sit inside your current program?
32. Who else in your organization would care about this?

### Price test

Do not ask "Would you pay?" in the abstract.

Use concrete packages:

"For a 4-6 week decision-readiness pilot with 8-15 founders, including platform access, onboarding, a facilitator dashboard, final DecisionRecords, pre/post measurement, and an institutional findings report, we are testing a price between USD 4,000 and USD 7,500. How would that land in your budget?"

Follow-ups:

33. Is that obviously too high, obviously reasonable, or something you would need to justify?
34. What budget category would this come from?
35. What would need to be included to make that price easy to defend?
36. If the pilot worked, what would an annual deployment need to include?

### Commitment test

37. Would you be willing to review a sample pilot agreement?
38. Is there a cohort in the next 90 days where this could fit?
39. Who else should join the next conversation?
40. If this is not a fit, who do you know who manages a cohort where this problem is more urgent?

## Evidence ladder

Rank each buyer conversation by the strongest signal reached.

1. Interesting, but no next step
2. Wants more information
3. Wants demo with team
4. Asks for pricing
5. Identifies a specific cohort
6. Introduces decision maker
7. Requests pilot proposal
8. Signs paid pilot
9. Renews or expands
10. Refers another buyer

## Red flags

Watch for these:

- They only care about founder-facing worksheets
- They do not measure founder progress today and do not feel pressure to do so
- They see reporting as irrelevant
- They have no recurring cohort
- They have no budget owner
- They say ChatGPT or Miro is good enough
- They want a full LMS, CRM, mentor platform, or application system before testing the decision workflow
- They love Paola's facilitation but do not care about VERTEX as repeatable software

## What to capture after each interview

Record these fields:

- Organization type
- Buyer title
- Cohort size
- Cohorts per year
- Current tools
- Current spend
- Main pain
- Reporting requirement
- Strongest VERTEX use case
- Objections
- Price reaction
- Decision process
- Next step
- Evidence ladder score
- Recommended follow-up
