# Founder Onboarding Script

## 5-minute founder intro

"VERTEX is here to help you make a clearer decision, not to judge your startup. We are going to capture where your thinking is today, then use VERTEX to examine the problem, stakeholders, adoption/resistance, economics, and final decision.

The important thing is not to sound polished. The important thing is to be honest about what you currently believe, what evidence you have, what is still uncertain, and what you would do next."

## What founders should bring

- current problem statement
- target customer
- user, payer, approver, and blocker if known
- current stakeholder list
- assumptions that must be true
- evidence already collected
- current price intuition
- main costs
- current decision: build, test, pivot, pause, or stop
- confidence score
- biggest uncertainty
- next test

## What baseline means

"The baseline is a timestamped snapshot of your current thinking. It is not a grade. It should not be perfect. We need the snapshot so we can see what changed by the end."

Do not overthink:

- exact wording
- perfect evidence
- complete stakeholder list
- polished financial model

Do be honest:

- what you know
- what you assume
- what you have not tested
- what would change your mind

## Privacy/data note

"Your team profile, baseline, artifacts, comments, rubric scores, and final DecisionRecord may be visible to the cohort facilitator and used in the program's outcome review. Avoid entering sensitive third-party personal data unless you have permission and a clear reason. VERTEX does not predict startup success or guarantee validation."

## How to move through Golden Path

1. Start a Decision Case.
2. Lock the baseline snapshot.
3. Work through problem framing.
4. Map the stakeholder system.
5. Review assumptions before predictive or financial processing.
6. Explore adoption/resistance hypotheses.
7. Review the financial scenario.
8. Save the DecisionRecord.
9. Review the Decision Memo.

## Why facilitator comments exist

"Facilitator comments are not penalties. They are a way to make important intervention points visible: missing evidence, unclear stakeholders, unresolved assumptions, financial gaps, or a decision that needs more support."

## Decision Memo explanation

"The Decision Memo is the artifact you should be able to keep. It explains the final decision, what changed from the baseline, what evidence supports the decision, what risk remains, and what you will test next."
