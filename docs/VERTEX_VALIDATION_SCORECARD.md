# VERTEX 4D - Pilot Validation Scorecard

## Purpose

This scorecard measures whether VERTEX improves founder decision quality and whether an institution values that improvement enough to pay.

It should be used before and after the VERTEX workflow.

## Founder baseline questions

Ask before the founder starts VERTEX:

1. What problem do you believe you are solving?
2. Who is the customer?
3. Who pays?
4. Who uses the solution?
5. Who can block adoption?
6. What assumptions must be true for this idea to work?
7. What evidence do you already have?
8. What price would you charge today?
9. What are your main variable and fixed costs?
10. What is your current decision: build, test, pivot, pause, or stop?
11. What is the biggest uncertainty?
12. What would you test next?

## Founder post-VERTEX questions

Ask after DecisionRecord is completed:

1. How would you define the problem now?
2. What stakeholders did you add or change?
3. Which assumptions are still unsupported?
4. What adoption or resistance risk matters most?
5. Did your pricing or financial view change?
6. What decision are you making now?
7. What evidence supports that decision?
8. What risk or unknown remains?
9. What is the next experiment?
10. What would make you reverse or update this decision?
11. Would you pay to use this without the cohort? Why or why not?
12. Would you recommend this to another founder? Why or why not?

## Independent scoring rubric

Two reviewers should score anonymized before/after submissions without knowing which version came first.

Use 1 to 5:

1 = vague or unsupported
2 = partially defined but weak
3 = acceptable for early stage
4 = strong and actionable
5 = unusually clear, evidence-aware, and decision-ready

| Dimension | What strong looks like |
|---|---|
| Problem definition | Specific, contextual, not just the proposed solution |
| Customer/buyer clarity | Separates user, payer, beneficiary, decision maker, and blocker |
| Stakeholder completeness | Identifies actors who can accelerate, resist, approve, fund, use, or block |
| Evidence quality | Separates facts, observations, interviews, assumptions, and inferences |
| Adoption reasoning | Explains why adoption or resistance might happen |
| Economic coherence | Price, costs, margin, break-even, and cash needs fit together logically |
| Uncertainty awareness | Names the unknowns that could change the decision |
| Decision quality | Decision follows from evidence and constraints |
| Next experiment quality | Has hypothesis, method, success threshold, and next action |
| Explainability | Founder can defend what changed and why |

## Founder-level success calculation

For each founder/team:

- Baseline total score
- Post-VERTEX total score
- Score delta
- Meaningful decision change: yes/no
- New stakeholder discovered: yes/no
- Price or economics changed: yes/no
- Next experiment improved: yes/no

Meaningful decision change means at least one of these changed:

- Problem definition
- Target customer
- Stakeholder priority
- Pricing
- Cost model
- Scope of pilot
- Go/pivot/pause/stop decision
- Next experiment

## Go/no-go thresholds after three institutional pilots

Strong GO:

- At least 2 of 3 pilots are paid
- More than 80% founder completion
- More than 50% meaningful decision change
- More than 70% hidden stakeholder/risk discovery
- Clear before/after quality improvement
- Program director uses dashboard or final report
- At least one renewal or expansion conversation

Weak or no-go:

- Buyers like the concept but avoid payment
- Founders enjoy it but decisions do not change
- Dashboard is ignored
- DecisionRecords are not useful
- Product requires Paola to manually carry every outcome
- Buyers only want generic workshops or consulting
