# VERTEX Buyer Demo Narrative

## One-page buyer story

Founder programs generate a lot of activity: workshops, mentor meetings, pitch practice, surveys, notes, and decks. But program directors often struggle to answer a sharper question:

Did founders make better decisions by the end of the cohort?

VERTEX is a decision-readiness system for entrepreneurship programs. It shows where founders' reasoning changes, where evidence is still weak, and where facilitators need to intervene, then turns those changes into a cohort-level record the institution can use.

The commercial value is best understood as three connected objects:

- Founder: `Decision Case` - a traceable decision file that helps a founder understand what changed and what to do next.
- Facilitator: `Intervention Radar` - a live view of which teams need help and why.
- Buyer: `Cohort Outcome Report` - an institutional artifact showing what changed during the program without claiming startup success.

The Decision Memo demonstrates individual value. The Cohort Outcome Report demonstrates institutional value.

One-sentence buyer promise:

Know where to intervene. Show what changed.

Core no-overclaim line:

VERTEX does not predict whether a startup will succeed. It makes the quality and evidence behind today's decision more visible.

## Pain VERTEX solves

Institutions need to show founder progress without relying on anecdotes, pitch-day polish, or scattered mentor notes. They need to know:

- Which teams have a locked baseline?
- Which teams still have unsupported assumptions?
- Which teams changed problem definition, stakeholder map, price, economics, or decision?
- Which cases need facilitator intervention?
- What can be shown to leadership, sponsors, funders, or boards?

VERTEX turns those questions into a visible cohort workflow.

For a program director, the most concrete job-to-be-done is:

"Tell my facilitator team where to intervene and why."

There is a second job at the end of the program:

"Show me what changed across this cohort in a form I can credibly use with leadership, funders, and the next program design."

## Why cohort-level decision quality matters

Early-stage programs should not promise that every startup will succeed. A stronger and more honest institutional outcome is that founders learn to make more defensible decisions before committing resources.

At cohort level, decision quality matters because it lets a program director compare progress without ranking founders by vanity metrics. The institution can see before/after evidence, rubric movement, unresolved gaps, and the final decision chain for each team.

## What the pilot proves

A paid VERTEX pilot should prove that:

- Founders can complete a traceable decision workflow.
- Facilitators can identify intervention needs during the cohort.
- Decision Memos are useful founder artifacts.
- Outcome Reports are useful institutional artifacts.
- Before/post rubric scores and changed decisions create a credible program-learning story.
- The buyer values decision quality enough to pay for another cohort.

It should not claim causal proof from a simple before/after design. A pilot can show measured change and buyer utility; it cannot prove that VERTEX alone caused all observed change.

## What VERTEX is not

VERTEX is not a worksheet. Worksheets collect answers; VERTEX reveals the decision system behind the answers.

VERTEX is not a chatbot. Chatbots produce conversation; VERTEX produces a traceable artifact chain.

VERTEX is not an LMS. It does not manage course content or attendance; it manages decision readiness.

VERTEX is not a CRM. It does not manage sales pipelines, mentor networks, or alumni relations.

VERTEX is not a startup prediction engine. It does not claim to validate companies, forecast success, or guarantee outcomes.

## Call to action

Run a paid 4-6 week Decision Quality Pilot with one founder cohort. Capture baselines, use VERTEX during facilitation, close each case with a DecisionRecord where possible, and review the final Outcome Report with the program director.

The commercial question is simple: does the buyer value visible, evidence-backed founder decision progress enough to repeat or expand the pilot?
