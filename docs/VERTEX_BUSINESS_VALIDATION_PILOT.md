# VERTEX 4D - Business Validation Pilot

## One-line thesis

VERTEX 4D helps entrepreneurship programs turn founder uncertainty into documented, evidence-backed decisions across a cohort.

## What VERTEX is

VERTEX is not another startup worksheet, chatbot, canvas, pricing calculator, or course module.

VERTEX is a guided decision-readiness system for early-stage founders. It takes a founder from an unclear idea to a traceable decision by forcing the team to examine five things:

1. What problem are we really solving?
2. Who is affected, involved, blocking, paying, using, or deciding?
3. What adoption or resistance might appear, and how uncertain is that hypothesis?
4. Do the price, costs, margin, break-even point, and financial projection make sense?
5. Given the evidence and uncertainty, what should we do next?

The output is a DecisionRecord: a final decision brief that links the decision back to assumptions, evidence, stakeholders, economics, risks, unknowns, and next experiments.

## The business problem

Founders often make expensive decisions on weak assumptions. They build before validating the problem, miss important stakeholders, confuse guesses with evidence, price from intuition, and fail to connect financial reality to strategic decisions.

Institutions have a second, more budgetable problem: entrepreneurship programs cannot always see, compare, or report how founders are improving their decision quality. Program directors need to know which teams are progressing, which teams are still making unsupported claims, what changed during the cohort, and what evidence can be shown to sponsors, funders, leadership, or boards.

## Core commercial claim to validate

The core claim is not:

"VERTEX has many tools in one place."

The stronger claim is:

"VERTEX creates an auditable chain from assumption to evidence to stakeholder system to economics to decision, so founders and the institutions supporting them can see not only what decision was reached, but whether that decision was justified."

## Initial target customer

The first customer should be an institution running recurring entrepreneurship programs, not an individual founder.

Best initial customer profile:

- Nonprofit incubator
- University entrepreneurship center
- Economic-development entrepreneurship program
- Pre-accelerator or early-stage founder program
- Consultant or facilitator who manages multiple founder cohorts

Ideal characteristics:

- Runs at least two structured cohorts per year
- Supports roughly 10 to 50 early-stage ventures annually
- Uses spreadsheets, Miro, Notion, mentor notes, LMS tools, or fragmented reporting today
- Needs to demonstrate founder progress to leadership, sponsors, funders, or a board
- Has a program owner who can approve a pilot without long enterprise procurement

Likely buyer titles:

- Incubator Director
- Accelerator Program Director
- Director of Entrepreneurship
- Entrepreneurship Center Manager
- Head of Venture Programs
- Director of Innovation and Entrepreneurship
- Economic Development Program Director
- Program Manager for founder cohorts

## Initial offer

The first product should be sold as a facilitated decision-readiness pilot powered by VERTEX.

Do not sell it yet as pure SaaS.
Do not sell it as a generic entrepreneurship course.
Do not sell it as AI startup advice.

Sell it as:

"A structured decision-readiness module for early-stage cohorts that measures how founders' problem definition, stakeholder awareness, assumptions, economics, and decisions change before and after the process."

## Pilot package

Recommended pilot size:

- 8 to 15 founders or teams
- 4 to 6 weeks
- 1 facilitator or program lead
- 1 institutional buyer or sponsor

Recommended pilot flow:

1. Buyer kickoff
2. Founder baseline capture
3. VERTEX guided workflow
4. DecisionRecord completion
5. Founder before/after scoring
6. Facilitator dashboard review
7. Institutional findings report
8. Buyer debrief and renewal conversation

These are hypotheses to test, not validated prices.

## What the pilot must prove

The pilot must prove business value, not just product satisfaction.

Founder-level proof:

- Founders complete the workflow
- Founders define the problem more clearly after VERTEX
- Founders identify stakeholders, blockers, or risks they had not considered
- Founders distinguish evidence from assumptions more clearly
- Founders change price, target customer, strategy, scope, or next experiment
- Founders leave with a decision they can explain and defend

Institution-level proof:

- Program director understands the dashboard
- Facilitator uses the dashboard to see where teams are stuck
- DecisionRecords are useful as cohort artifacts
- Before/after measurement helps the institution show program impact
- Buyer sees this as improving program quality, reporting, or mentor consistency
- Buyer is willing to pay for another cohort or annual deployment

## Strong validation signals

Continue investing aggressively only if the next pilots show signals like these:

| Signal | Strong validation threshold |
|---|---|
| Founder completion | More than 80% complete the core journey without excessive intervention |
| Decision impact | More than 50% make a meaningful change to problem, customer, strategy, price, economics, or next experiment |
| Hidden-risk discovery | More than 70% identify at least one important assumption, stakeholder, or risk they had not considered |
| Independent quality scoring | Before/after submissions improve across most rubric categories |
| Buyer usefulness | Program director actively uses dashboard/report, not only founder-facing pages |
| Commercial demand | At least 2 of first 3 institutional pilots are paid |
| Renewal | At least 1-2 buyers want another cohort or annual deployment |
| Price resistance | Objections are about budget timing/mechanics, not lack of value |
| Referral | Buyer introduces VERTEX to another program director |

## Weak validation signals

These are not enough:

- "This is interesting"
- "I like the idea"
- "Founders enjoyed it"
- "Send me more information"
- "Maybe next semester"
- Founders love it but no institution will pay
- Institution wants only consulting and ignores the software

## Go/no-go decision after three pilots

Go if:

- At least 2 of 3 pilots are paid
- Founders measurably improve decision quality
- Buyers use or value the dashboard/report
- At least one buyer wants to repeat or expand
- VERTEX is valued as a repeatable system, not only as Paola's personal facilitation

Pause or reposition if:

- Founders like it but institutions will not pay
- Institutions pay only for facilitation and do not care about software
- The workflow is too hard to complete without heavy manual support
- DecisionRecords are not useful to buyers
- Buyers see it as interchangeable with Strategyzer, Miro, ChatGPT, or existing accelerator tools

## What not to build before validation

Do not build these now:

- Full accelerator management system
- Application management
- Mentor matching
- CRM
- Alumni community
- Investor deal-flow tools
- Full LMS
- White labeling
- Enterprise SSO
- More generic AI features
- Claims that VERTEX predicts startup success

The product should not claim to predict success. The defensible promise is:

"VERTEX helps founders make a more defensible decision before committing more resources."

## Immediate next milestone

The next milestone is not more features.

The next milestone is:

Three institutions paid VERTEX to run a real decision-readiness pilot; founders measurably improved the quality of their decisions; at least two institutions wanted to use it again.
